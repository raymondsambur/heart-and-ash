# M6: a living world

Built on the branch `world-living` (from `main` at the smart-placement merge), written in the owner's main session. It answers one list of
requests: the wall must be one closed loop with a single gate, roads must come from where people walk, the map must be bigger, foragers must
pick real plants, and trees must be cut and regrow.

Done when: the wall is a loop, tiles that are walked wear into roads and unused ones grow back, a new village starts on a 160 x 160 map, foragers
stand at a bush or in the grass and take from it, and a felled tree is a stump, then a sapling, then a tree again; all of it deterministic,
saved, sent to the client, and measured in the soak.

## What you get

### Living ground (`sim/flora.ts`, `shared/data/flora.ts`, `FLORA`)

Plants are tiles of the map that the sim now changes in place (`st.flora` lists the tiles that differ from the generated map, `st.chops` the
blows a tree has taken).

- **Trees.** A woodcutter walks to a real tree near the forest site, stands beside it, faces it and chops. Four chops fell it (`trees_felled`
  in the stats) and leave a **stump**, which becomes a **sapling** after 12 days and a tree again after 30 more. Nothing grows in winter.
- **Bushes.** A forager picks from a bush (1.3 food), flowers (1) or grass (0.6), in that order of preference; herb gatherers take flowers first.
  A picked bush is a **bare bush** and bears again after 10 days. Thatch is cut from grass.
- Building on a stump, a sapling or a bare bush is allowed (the plant is cleared); walls are laid through trees and brush.
- The new object codes are `O.Stump = 16`, `O.Sapling = 17`, `O.BareBush = 18`, with art in `client/art/flora.ts`.

### Roads from footsteps (`sim/roads.ts`, `BAL.roads`)

Every tile a person steps on is counted (`st.traffic`, a float per tile). Each game day the counts decay (x0.96) and open ground (grass, plains,
forest floor, sand, hill) with a count of 55 or more and nothing on it becomes a **dirt road** (`st.ground` keeps the terrain that differs from
the generated map). A dirt road is faster to walk on (speed 1.2), so people choose it, which keeps it busy. A road tile whose count stays under
18 for 10 days (`st.quiet`) goes back to what the map had there (grass for the map's own roads). Buildings, fields and walls clear the ground
under them. The client draws the worn ground from the `ground` list of the view.

### The wall is a loop with one gate (`shared/data/walls.ts`, `sim/walls.ts`)

- `planRing` plans one rectangle around everything the village has, with a margin, of whole four-tile pieces, inside what may be built, with every
  slot on ground a wall may stand on. The plan is made once (`st.ring`) and kept.
- The pieces touch end to end with no gap. A wall may only go on a slot that joins a finished or planned piece (the first goes beside the gate),
  so the loop grows from the gate outwards. Other buildings may not take the empty slots (`reservedSlots`), and buildings that belong inside
  the wall are scored down when they are outside it.
- The **gate** (`gate` build kind, art `gateway`) goes on the side the road comes from and is the only way through; there is exactly one. The
  rest of the loop is blocked for walking; the gateway tiles stay open.
- `sim/walls.ts` `isClosed` checks the loop by flood fill from the green. A closed loop adds a bonus to the village's defence in a raid
  (`BAL.defence.loop`), and the Manage tab shows the progress (`built / total`, gate, closed).
- The suggested rules build walls while under 60 (`walls < 60`) and one gate (`gates < 1`); orders for walls snap to the slots, and the
  placement preview in the client shows the slot.

### A bigger map (`MAP_SIZE = 160`)

New villages are made on 160 x 160 tiles (the first maps were 96 x 96). The generator offsets everything so the old design sits in the middle: the
starting village is the same village, moved to the middle, and the extra land has more forest, bushes and stone. Terrain, objects and the
village stay connected (checked for four seeds). A snapshot stores its `size`, so an older village keeps its old map: the host keeps the size in
`village.<id>.size` (new villages get `MAP_SIZE`, existing ones 96).

On a bigger map the sites (forest, meadow, stream) are the ones nearest the village, not the first in raster order; the 96 map keeps its order so
saves keep their site ids.

## Speed

The 160 map has 2.8 times the tiles of the 96 one and the wall made paths longer, so the first version was far too slow (one soak seed took
391 s). Three changes fixed it:

- the pathfinder keeps its scratch memory between calls (stamps instead of clearing an array),
- the cost of entering a tile is cached in a `Float64Array` (`touch(i)` after every change of an object or of the terrain, `refreshCosts()`
  after buildings change; the cache is double precision so it gives exactly the numbers the live function gave),
- sites are collected nearest first, which also shortens trips.

Seed 3 with the suggested rules now runs 200 game days in 29 s of CPU. The bench (`scripts/bench.ts`, 160 map) is within 1.0 to 1.2x of the previous numbers for 12 to 300 people.

## Guard duty (follow-up)

Watch duty used to happen on the plaza, and a guard was just a title (best skill fighting, level 3 or more) with no effect on what they did.
`sim/duty.ts` now sets it out:

- **Where.** Finished watch posts and the gate are `post` sites. A watcher stands at the gate first, then at the watch post with the fewest
  others on it; a patrol goes to the stop it visited longest ago among the gate, the posts and the green, so it walks a round. With neither a
  gate nor a post, duty is still on the plaza.
- **How many.** The old slots stay (2 a post, 2 more while a raid is expected, at most 6). The gate adds one, and with two guards (four, two)
  and a gate or post there is a patrol (`patrol:round`). Outside a raid the gate and the patrol together never take more than half the adults.
- **Who.** A guard scores duty jobs 2.2 times higher, so guards keep watch and patrol before foraging and wood cutting. The lean ends while
  there are under 6 days of food or under 1.5 days of firewood: then guards go back to the work the village needs. The Defence priority still
  scales the watch and the patrol.
- **Measured** (seed 3, rules only, days 60 to 160): guards spent 32% of their hours standing watch and 6% patrolling, against 3% chopping wood;
  of the hours on duty, 58% were at the gate, 24% at a watch post, 9% on the green during a patrol and the rest walking between. Soak, 160 map,
  8 seeds, 5 years: suggested rules 8/8 survive (average 37.6 people, it was 36.0), scripted manager 8/8 (39.6, it was 38.1).

## Walls across streams (follow-up)

Iron Village could not plan a wall at all: a stream crossed every possible loop, and the wall rules failed without a word. What changed:

- **Shallow water takes a wall piece** (not a gate, and not deep water, rock or paving). A stream is already no way through, so a piece standing
  in it closes the line.
- **The loop counts the ground.** `isClosed` treats water and rock as barriers, so a loop that ends at a stream is closed, and the village
  reports closed without every slot built (two slots across a stream that nobody can reach are not needed). Once closed, further wall orders
  are refused with "already closed" instead of failing quietly.
- **Why a wall cannot go up is said** in the Work tab of Manage (`policy.wallBlock`, a new field of the view): no route is clear and what blocks
  the best one (`ringFailure`), or the next piece cannot be reached, or a wall would cut the village off. It is not saved: the rules ask
  again and it comes back within a day.
- **A haul deadlock is fixed.** A stage needed all its material, but a load was only called for when a whole unit was missing, so a piece 0.9 stone
  short waited for ever (it held Iron Village's wall at 34 of 36 for over 100 days). A shortfall that holds a stage up now gets its load.
- **A loop must not shut the village off.** Finishing walls showed the cost of the loop: with a single gate, trips to the forest on the far
  side of the ring were three times as long, and in one case a stream beside the wall left the gate opening onto the bank the forests were not on, so
  people outside could not get back in and the village starved. The gate is now put where the trips out are shortest (`bestGate`: the slot with
  the lowest average of green-to-gate plus gate-to-place over the forests, meadows, streams, quarries and the road out, weighted towards food),
  and a loop that leaves a whole kind of place unreachable, or fewer than 85% of them, is not taken. A search that finds no loop is not repeated
  for 15 days, and looks for a gate on at most 24 rectangles.
- **People arrive at the water.** The spot a person walks to near a site was chosen at random each time they planned again (they plan every few
  seconds), so a starving villager could keep walking between neighbouring tiles and never begin to drink. It is now the same tile for a person and a
  site. A place on the map with a building on it (a wall across a stream site) is no longer offered.
- **Measured** (soak, 160 map, 8 seeds, 5 years): suggested rules 8/8 survive, average 42.4 people (it was 37.6 with guard duty); scripted manager
  8/8, 41.1 (39.6). On Iron Village's own save, 120 days: the wall is 36 of 36 and closed by day 80 and the village holds 29 to 30 people.
  The golden run changed (people kept near each other chat more: 46 chats where it was 23).

## Trades (follow-up)

People used to take any job off the open board, with skill only a soft preference, so a woodcutter could stand watch and a guard could forage. Now each adult has a **trade** (the title in the villager list): the one the village gave them (`Person.trade`, optional, so old saves read as before), else their best skill once it reaches level 3. `tradeOf` in `people.ts`; the rules are in `sim/src/trades.ts`.

- A job that asks for a skill goes only to someone of that trade. A person with no trade yet (best skill under 3) may take any job, and finds a trade by it.
- **Help when the trade is busy.** Each refresh marks a job `help` when it has more open places than free people of its trade (none, or all busy at something else); others may take it, and only it. This was the second step: fully strict trades left whole trades idle and cost about a third of the population in the soak.
- **Emergencies.** Food jobs open to all below 3 days of food, wood below 0.75 days of firewood, and the watch and patrol during a raid (defence is whoever stands watch).
- **Builders only** for building, demolishing and burying (the funeral job asks for `building`).
- **Enough of each trade.** Where the board has too few people in a trade (fewer than half the places its jobs offer, up to three), the adult best at it is given the trade, from a trade with people to spare, once each; the feed says so. Skilled jobs get room for everyone of the trade (up to six).
- Soak, 8 seeds, 4 years, population at the end: rules only 34.1 before, 32.4 now; with the manager 36.9 before, 32.6 now. (Fully strict, without help: 23.0 and 26.9.)

## Trees beside the wall (follow-up)

Trees standing against a finished wall showed their crowns over it (and the wall over their trunks). A wall order now cuts the trees within two tiles of the piece as well as under it, and the bushes on the tile next to it (`clearBrush` with a margin, `WALL_CLEAR = 2`; flowers beside it stay). The first version cut one tile and left the bushes, and crowns and bushes still reached the wall, and once a day `clearAlongWalls` does the same along every wall that stands or is going up, which also tidies villages that built their wall before this. What is cut stays bare, as before. The tests are in `walls.test.ts`.

## The watch outside the gate (follow-up)

The watcher at the gate now stands outside the wall, two tiles beyond the gate piece on the side away from the village's houses (`outsideGate` in `sites.ts`; the gate door itself when there are no houses to tell in from out). The patrol round still goes by it.

## Limits

- The live village (Plum Ford, 96 x 96) keeps its small map. Walls placed before this change stay as loose pieces; they are not part of the
  ring, so the new loop is planned around them and may need some cleared first.
- Only one gate per loop and one loop per village. Walls are not repaired or upgraded.
- There is one kind of worn road (dirt); stone roads are not made by walking.
- Silver still has few uses.
